# CCH-INN-STORY-001 — The Room That Was Dry (Complete Sample Side Arc)

**2026-11-14. Status:** Original narrative/quest **prototype on paper only**; no new canonical NPC, client, line, recipe or implementation approval. Edith and Thomas remain proposed residents of future Wicker Cove. Requires district, woodworking, contracts and persistent inn room before production.
**Literary direction:** Plain, specific writing; quiet, honest stakes; people decide for themselves; player choice leaves visible, useful changes. No skill or morality score that judges whose needs matter more.

## Why this is a prototype
The game promises structural repairs and guestroom decorating, but needs an **example where a paid job, genuine resource tradeoff, character relationship, interior customization and a later human consequence all belong to the same story**. This side arc provides one. It cannot alter Maren's minute book, vicar register, Davy, postman hand, original endings or fog cause.

## Cast
- **Edith Vale, candidate innkeeper:** practical, often tired, not helpless. Needs one roof/wall leak fixed and a room made useful; willing to pay for real work.
- **Thomas Bellweather, candidate storekeeper:** keeps fair accounts and orders hardware. Refuses to pretend he can supply goods before shipment actually arrives.
- **Player:** farmer with limited logs, planks, tools, time and other priorities.
- **First guest:** an original unnamed traveling bookkeeper or schoolteacher, selected later after owner cast decision. Brief must be specific enough to serve as a fair furnishing task.

## Act I — Spring: 'The Leak'
**Trigger:** first or subsequent inn visit, where inn exists and upper room has not been structurally repaired. No required quest gate to enter town.
**Scene:**

> The upstairs ceiling had three dark patches.
>
> Edith moved a bucket away from the window.
>
> 'That one hasn't dripped since Monday,' she said.
>
> A drop fell into it.
>
> She looked at the ceiling.
>
> 'I wasn't counting on it remembering.'

**Work inspection:** 1 short interaction makes material requirements visible, including a real quantity of planks **only if logs/workbench chain is implemented**. Display exact costs/prerequisites based on actual inventory/Carpentry. No fake planks in a build still using generic wood.
**Player approach:**
A. **Quote a paid repair:** Edith agrees to transparent coins/materials if she can afford it. Player accepts only after seeing actual terms.
B. **Offer to trade:** Edith can provide an actual resource/service (limited lodging, meal orders or raw goods) if economy implements it; no made-up free upgrade.
C. **Ask for time:** quote saved for later; no quest failure or negative friendship.
D. **Just visit:** discuss inn, buy food, leave. Town retains utility.
**Immediate:** promise state only if actual contract accepted. Edith may be relieved but room is still wet until repaired.

## Act II — Summer: 'The Boards We Have'
**Trigger:** player has plausible building materials and active repair quote, or chooses farm project.
**Material dilemma:** same prepared planks can repair the farmhouse or inn. Both investments have real value and no alignment value.
**Choices:**
1. **Farm first.** Own roof/repair improves when actually completed. Edith patches with buckets and may ask another carpenter later. The contract remains open only if enough time/capacity remains. She does not tell the player they're selfish.
2. **Inn first for payment.** Player provides planks, spends bounded work/time, Edith contributes agreed materials and pays once. One roof patch visibly changes and interior leak disappears; player delays own farm improvement.
3. **Shared work.** Agree on split materials and labor if mechanics support. Return after gathering supplies; partner must genuinely contribute. Could reduce cash reward but open guest-room collaboration.
4. **No job.** Let owner continue solving it. A few weeks later Edith might have patched one corner; no automatic complete renovation or moral reprimand.
**Optional hidden roll:** only uncertain roof inspection or hard-to-reach repair with appropriate existing Carpentry and equipment, warned before attempting. Failure increases extra work or yields an alternate safer fix. A failure must not permanently close the room nor strip essential resources without warning.
**Actual crafting:** player must fell appropriate trees in forest, carry raw logs and hew planks at physical workbench; no anvil unless an appropriate metal fastening component is genuinely part of recipe. Avoid turning each board into long repetitive animation.

**Illustrative completion scene:**
> Rain hit the roof just after dusk.
>
> Edith stood in the upstairs doorway and listened.
>
> The bucket stayed empty.
>
> 'Well,' she said.
>
> After a moment she lifted it and carried it downstairs.
>
> 'Might put something useful in this now.'

**No player present:** Edith can notice roof on her own afterward if state is repaired; no mandatory congratulatory cutscene.

## Act III — Autumn: 'Someone May Stay'
**Trigger:** repaired room actually available.
**Edith:** 'There's a guest coming. Not a rich one. Wants a clean bed and somewhere to write. I can give them the bed. Could you help with the rest?'
**Objective room client brief:** at minimum: a usable bed, unobstructed door/path, a safe light source. Guest preferences: writing surface and one chair; optional cozy extras such as curtain/rug. All items must exist or be added in a separately approved furniture slice.
**Player agency:** arrange room themselves, offer raw furniture, request help for placement, or decline decoration while inn still uses basic functional room. A room is usable as long as safety minimums met; don't force decorative taste grading.
**Evaluation:** judge exact item/layout conditions, not a hidden 'beauty meter'; two levels: usable requirements met, preferred comforts met. The owner can still finish basics if player leaves.
**Narrative feedback:**
- Only minimum: Edith: 'They slept dry. That's what mattered.'
- Minimum + writing surface: Edith: 'The visitor stayed up writing. I heard the chair scrape before dawn. Said the table was just the height.'
- Declined room styling but repaired shell: Edith: 'I've got a bed in there now. Borrowed one from the back room. It'll do.'
**Save:** room furniture positions and completed contract once; avoid rewarding repeatedly by moving same chair.
**Do not** instantly fill every boarded room with tourists.

## Act IV — Winter: 'A Name in the Register'
**Trigger:** guest actually stayed; reference the inn's ordinary guest book, not the vicar's parish **register** or any secret Rook witness record; change title if needed to avoid lore confusion.
**Scene:**
> Edith turned a page in the inn ledger.
>
> 'They left early.'
>
> 'Everything all right?'
>
> 'Said the room was warm. Left the window shut. I'd have done the same.'
>
> She set a folded note on the counter.
>
> 'They asked me to thank whoever fixed the roof.'

**Potential variations:**
- Guest had work table: a folded appreciation about good table/light, no romance or mystical message.
- Usable basic room: short thanks for dry bed.
- Repair never occurred: inn still taking guests downstairs as possible, the upstairs room remains not ready. This isn't a failure screen; Edith has her own responses.
- Player visited other businesses instead: one real merchant callback may mention goods purchased, but no fictional credit for inn.
**Story secret restraint:** the note contains no definitive Wicker Cove fog origin, supernatural curse, Agnes secret correspondence or post_hand handwriting reveal.

## Act V — Second spring and after ending: 'The Lit Window'
**Trigger:** only after actual repaired and currently occupied room, or visible modest repair, persisting between saves.
**Visual:** warm upstairs window above an otherwise worn inn. In winter a little condensation; in summer curtains may stir on fair non-foggy interiors while permanent exterior coastal fog stays.
**Quiet line:** Edith, sweeping the steps: 'Room's full this week. People still need somewhere dry.'
**If never repaired:** the same inn remains operational and may have a bucket and boarded shutter; no ominous 'the village died' implication.
**After any of three existing endings:** the room state persists and Edith responds to normal business; Rook bell story doesn't reset it, and Wicker Cove fog doesn't vanish.
**Diary:** 1–2 factual lines, only if the player truly did labor or furnish room; no assigned love for the town.

## Core branches and mechanical outcomes (design table)

| Branch | Immediate cost | Tangible result | Delayed callback | Later possibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Repair own home first | logs/planks for farm | improved personal shelter | Edith uses interim bucket/another worker | inn commission may remain |
| Paid inn repair | material + time | room dry; coins once | guest can stay | furnish for one brief |
| Shared improvement | negotiated materials + effort | roof/room stage | Edith refers to co-work | optional guestroom design |
| Defer | none now | no automatic repair | owner keeps working/patches small part | job later if still viable |
| Repair only, no furniture | resources of structural repair | dry usable room | Edith supplies minimal bed | guest stays with modest feedback |
| Repair and meet brief | actual furniture + placement | room meets stated preferences | guest-specific grounded response | optional returning guest/room edits |

## Design to implementation mapping (not code)
1. **Existing order system** carries repair commission acceptance, payment and optional expiry.
2. **Wood resources** carry forest logs / hewn planks with real item and recipe conditions.
3. **Local business stage** records dry upper room once; artwork changes.
4. **One room layout** records items/rotation/clear path; reuse Godot furnishing/panel components where possible.
5. **Social outcome** records witnessed/delivered job with proven actor and one later message.
6. **Diary** stores earned memory line in player handwriting, not Agnes's.
7. **Original story** runs independently with untouched endings.

## Tests

- Try furniture placement blocking door: objective warning; player can correct.
- Guest review only actual supplied items; no invisible beauty score.
- Farm-only/never-in-town save: no fabricated inn dialogue/diary.
- Davy/Maren/postman/vicar/chapter events unchanged; post-ending room persists.
- Weather graphics: interior dry when repaired, permanent outside fog at Cove.
