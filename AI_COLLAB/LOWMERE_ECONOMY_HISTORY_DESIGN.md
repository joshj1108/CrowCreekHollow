# CCH-LOWMERE-ECON-001 — Lowmere's Decline, Working Economy and Harbor Recovery
**Draft:** 2026-11-14. **State:** Design proposal only; no gameplay, lore, asset or implementation approval.

## Established canon to preserve
- Lowmere is a dreary, failing seaside fishing village down the mountain road from Crow Creek Hollow, veiled in a permanent 24/7 fog bank.
- Crow Creek Hollow is the last inhabited farm before the wilderness, where the established treks begin.
- Existing main story involves Agnes and Elias Rook, Old Maren, Davy Tull, the vicar, the postman, the mill vote and E. Pell's letter; farm's keeper role and three endings must remain intact.
- Existing farm daily jobs, stand trading/demand, walker requests, town letter, cellar, cooking, fishing, land deed and property taxes must be integrated without doubling rewards or contradicting chronology.
- New names Edith Vale, Gideon Pike, Thomas Bellweather and Martha Fenwick are proposed resident concepts, not canon.

## Core premise (PROPOSED, not established backstory)
Lowmere was formerly a modest viable fishing harbor, not a giant city. Over the years the local fleet shrank, reliable catches and trade declined, buildings aged, household finances deteriorated, and families departed. A persistent fog hangs over it; its origin and precise chronology are unresolved. Real economic problems, human decisions, and possible unexplained maritime changes coexist. **Do not definitively blame the permanent fog, the Hollow, or Agnes's bell for the economic decline without separate story approval.**

### Six pressures with playable symptoms
| Cause/pressure | Visible or spoken evidence | Gameplay use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poor or unreliable fish landings | Empty fish-market trays, idle crewmembers, unsold fishing gear | Fishing jobs, safe ground surveys, catch delivery, optional crew trips |
| Broken harbor equipment | Damaged gangways, unused moorings, rotten nets | Carpentry/metalwork/fiber supply and staged repairs |
| Uneven food supply | Villagers relying on tins, dried fish, imports | Demand for inland vegetables, eggs, preserved food and inn catering |
| Store stock shortages | Sparse general-store shelving and irregular shipments | Trade orders, supply consistency, repairs and inventory unlocks |
| Falling inn occupancy | One or more boarded guestrooms, small common-room staff | Room repair and one client decorating commission, plus guest food demand |
| Declining routes | Few regular visiting merchants, late coastal deliveries | Future crewed coastal supply trips, first modeled as off-map destinations |

No requirement to model a city economy, dynamic population, unemployment or market speculation.

## Production network
**Crow Creek Hollow farm → Lowmere:** vegetables, orchard fruit, eggs/milk, preserved meals, timber, firewood, basic crafted parts. Owner's current stock/skills must drive achievable order categories.
**Lowmere → farm:** coastal fish and shellfish candidates, salt/preservation materials, nets/rope/hardware, specialized seeds/furnishings, rumors and occasional guests' requests. Introduce item kinds only after checking existing item design and economy.
**Lowmere fishing → fish market → inn/store:** one repaired fish market improves storage or steady demand, not whole-port magical prosperity.
**Coastal trade → off-map ports:** later optional procurement/sales with saved manifests and a voyage choice; other towns need no playable maps in v1.0.

## Repair progression with limits
The town has **localized improvements**, not an all-or-nothing village meter:
- **Inn**: fix one guest room → allow themed design and an actual guest → improve meal/customer demand.
- **Fish market**: restore cold storage → supply more kinds of seafood and fulfill a higher-value order.
- **General store**: repair shelving/storeroom → make a few useful items available and create new contracts.
- **Harbor**: make one pier safe → unlock one short NPC-led fishing trip.
- A subtle town-scene acknowledgment can show residents doing meaningful follow-up labor. Remain fogbound, aged, partly abandoned even at late mastery.
- **Provisional limiting rule:** every building restoration must offer one functional unlock, one visible world change and one personal character acknowledgment. No required village-wide completion for existing endings.

## Historic timeline to develop with owner (no dates canonized)
1. **Working-harbor era:** reliable fishing and coastal exchange, enough economic life to support an inn and store.
2. **Declining-catch era:** weaker grounds, storms/damage, expensive repairs and departing crews. Fog is persistent now; timing and cause unknown.
3. **Current era:** fishing fleet too small, market half-empty, empty rooms, service providers still working; inland farming and boat repairs can matter.
4. **Player era:** selective restoration stabilizes essential services and opens modest maritime paths, never instantly cures the village.
- Compatibility gate: compare all proposed eras against Agnes's approximately thirty-year disappearance, six farm transfers, mill vote, 31-to-4 letter, town clerk and existing second-year chronology. Avoid defining whether old families owned fleets or caused fog until audited.

## Distinctive resident attitudes to crisis (candidates)
- **Edith Vale, inn:** practical optimism tempered by debt and maintenance; will accept help but still runs the business herself.
- **Gideon Pike, fishing crew:** believes reliable working gear and cooperation matter more than speeches; knows route risks.
- **Martha Fenwick, fish market:** prioritizes selling catch, preserving food, and feeding families through winter; skeptical of easy solutions.
- **Thomas Bellweather, general store:** optimistic about supplies and exchange but must manage low stock; avoids exploitative buy/sell dynamics.
- **Existing Maren/vicar/postman:** have differing perspectives on old farm/keeper story and responsibility, subject to original Folk scenes. Preserve their own schedules/quests and endings.
- A believable village needs competing viewpoints and ordinary conflict, not an entire cast of secret villains.

## Early, mid, late-game Lowmere content (illustrative)
**First visit:** fogbound village, live fish market and sparse shop, one manageable farm goods request and one small boat/dock sight; no compulsory mystery cutscene or hard gate to essential shops.
**Early relationship:** repeated produce/fish contracts, first repair quote from Edith, visible lived-in shop restocking; bounded rewards.
**Mid:** complete inn room repair and first decor request, useful fish-market preservation upgrades, occasional named guest or merchant.
**Later:** mend one dock and accompany Gideon for one working fishing trip if maritime feature passes prototype; discover a fishing ground; sample coastal manifest trade.
**Long-term:** optional improvement projects, recurring practical contracts, winter resilience, social acknowledgment and post-ending free play; no need to fix every boarded house.

## Economic balancing and risk controls
- Current game already has farm stand demand/glut and a rotating daily job with pay/XP. New contracts **must share fulfillment/reward logic**, prevent duplicate turn-ins and have an explicitly bounded profit premium.
- Early orders must use obtainable goods in appropriate seasons, achievable quantities and no punitive expirations. Player can decline and ignore deliveries.
- Avoid perpetual demand that instantly pays for all upgrades, and avoid turning the farm into a mandatory industrial production grind.
- Repairs may involve owner-contributed materials and a single primary skill (Carpentry) plus optional specialty supplier. Coin, XP and reputation rewards capped; construction time is not excessive.
- Fog is always present at town-wide scale but view readability and accessibility maintained. Weather/seasonal events may vary density; no blanket dark gameplay tint.
- The ocean is not the default solution to fixing all problems. Ordinary agricultural and restorative accomplishments stay valuable.
