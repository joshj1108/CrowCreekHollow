# CCH-COMMUNITY-CALENDAR-001 — Twelve Months of Ordinary Life and Remembered Choices

**2026-11-14. Status: Design-only seasonal/ambient narrative bible.** No new holidays, meetings, NPC schedules or buffs are authorized by these suggestions. The original `DESIGN.md` and `StoryAct` calendar is authoritative.

## Intent
A living world does not need twelve mandatory festivals or a quest for each new month. Make the community recognizable through ordinary repeating work: autumn repairs, winter stores, spring seedlings, summer fishing, post deliveries, rooms made ready for strangers. A few **conditional** lines and altered objects create the sense of a story continuing while the farmer is elsewhere.

### Existing calendar guardrails
- Starts **October 1**, first autumn; in-game months use real lengths and normal 365-day cycle.
- **October 31 All Hallows:** existing road lanterns and reduced dark sanity cost.
- **November 10 First Frost:** existing warmth/temperature event for three days.
- **December 21 Longest Night:** existing amplified nighttime Sanity mechanic.
- **January 1 Winter Feast:** existing 2 grilled fish + bandage reward; NOT a rendered Wicker Cove festival.
- **March 1 Spring Thaw:** existing two-day watered-crop growth benefit.
- **April 20 Planting Fair:** existing 5 turnip seeds; NOT an already-implemented town fair.
- **May28 approx day240:** existing E. Pell letter arrives. Fixed original story.
- **June21 Midsummer Heat:** existing sunny days for three days, inland farm; Wicker Cove always fogbound.
- **June22 approx day265:** first *eligible* orchard trek after required letter beat may trigger water rise; not automatic at midnight.
- **July12 approx day285:** earliest Hollow's copied knock and only after actual orchard beat +4 days; not fixed-night guarantee.
- **July27 approx day300:** earliest mill re-vote with Maren book and vicar register.
- **September 22 Harvest Moon:** existing 2 turnip hashes +10 Sanity, original meeting fallback evaluated **after** relevant threshold if not held.
- **October5 second calendar autumn day370:** Davy goes only if prior orchard beat and main story still open; found 20:00 to before 01:00.
- **October31 second autumn around day396:** second All Hallows / crow bell progression if requirements allow.
**The calendar below is atmospheric design, not a replacement for that list.**

## Month-by-month proposal

### October — Making a place
**Farm:** first roof/field/food tasks, forest's edge seen, wood stacked before rain. First day/night and Maren day4 escort remain unchanged.
**Lane:** Maren may offer **practical** advice after legitimate meeting; postman brings ordinary mail; Davy available only after day20.
**Wicker Cove (if map is present in future playthroughs):** residents waterproof boats and inn, move crates off damp ground.
**Microstory:** Maren notices whether one board truly stayed dry, not whether player seems virtuous.
**No-player path:** she gets back to her own mill chores.
**Sound/sight:** gulls farther off in fog, loose hinge, distant woodpecker, drying herbs. Not every creak supernatural.

### November — First Frost, second thoughts
**Farm:** colder nights, stores, later autumn harvest. Existing First Frost Nov10 respected.
**People:** Davy mentions his mother's caution and Pip; Maren speaks about damp wood, not yet necessarily the vote.
**Town:** Thomas counts dwindling imported goods; Martha begins preserving fish; Edith checks roof leak (candidate residents).
**Microstory:** a realistic paid errand or a simple refusal. If player shared resources, winter will still require preparation, but not punitive ruin.
**No-player path:** villagers make imperfect arrangements amongst themselves.
**Sight:** bare boughs against inn glass, wind in rope, market awning weighted down.

### December — The long dark
**Farm:** hearth, fish, preserved food, original Longest Night Dec21; no extra compulsory chase.
**People:** vicar puts out hymn books, postman arrives damp, Maren stays practical.
**Town:** fewer working boats, Edith keeps a hearth warm even with empty rooms.
**Microstory:** winter neighbor supply request (proposed) offers help/trade/reserve/another solution; one later callback rather than immediate moral label.
**No-player path:** supplies may come late or from someone else.
**Sight:** lamplight in fog, warm breath, a dry workbench if built.

### January — The ordinary kindness of surviving
**Farm:** existing Winter Feast Jan1 reward, ongoing animal/fuel chores.
**People:** Davy finds games to play in cold, postman talks about muddy lanes; Maren offers a kitchen habit.
**Town:** fish preservation, store's salted goods and inn soup are practical, not a giant feast cutscene.
**Microstory:** one neighbor remembers a genuine December transaction.
**No-player path:** the town still feeds itself imperfectly.
**Sight:** steam at windows, ice at pond edges only if the actual weather supports it.

### February — The first plans
**Farm:** repair gear, choose materials to save for thaw, prep seed money.
**People:** vicar inventories service books, Maren counts supplies for Ruth.
**Town:** Thomas revises orders; Gideon considers boat work as spring approaches.
**Microstory:** player may reserve wood for own house, prepare repairs for someone else or ignore notices. Resource outcomes actually matter.
**No-player path:** owners can patch roofs temporarily.
**Sight:** rain softening frozen paths, wet work clothes.

### March — What returns
**Farm:** existing Spring Thaw March1, plant crops, visit orchard paths when eligible.
**People:** Davy looks for spring fish; Maren sees the first berries; postman carries seed catalogs.
**Town:** first Wicker Cove arrival is **optional** once map works; some arrive to sell, repair, investigate, or simply purchase goods.
**Microstory:** a resident gives a useful warning, then returns to work; player can create their own first impression.
**No-player path:** farm-only play remains rich.
**Sight:** thawed mud, unpainted boards, salt on shopfront glass.

### April — Work before summer
**Farm:** existing Planting Fair April20 gives 5 turnip seeds; event does not prove in-town stalls exist.
**People:** vicar notices empty seats at chapel; Davy hunts a better skipping stone.
**Town:** Edith wants the upstairs room dry; Thomas needs reliable vegetables; Martha considers summer ice storage.
**Microstory:** buyer's return basket or a paid quote, not five simultaneous civic obligations.
**No-player path:** shop imports what it can and sells imperfect goods.
**Sight:** laundry lines on clear days inland; fog remains near harbor.

### May — When the town writes
**Farm:** flowers, orchard, first longer outings; fixed day240 E. Pell parish letter ~May28.
**People:** existing walkers react to the letter in their original `StoryAct.SHOWN` scenes. Additional lines after it can depend on actual town relationship, never replace letter.
**Town:** a worker remembers Rook farm by old sale or a practical matter; no universal conspiracy.
**Microstory:** two conflicting fog accounts *optional*, not necessary for original Rook evidence.
**No-player path:** the letter still arrives and main story proceeds.
**Sight:** sharp inland sun, permanent pale harbor air.

### June — What the land provides
**Farm:** summer growing, existing Midsummer Heat from June21, orchard event conditional from day265.
**People:** Gideon and Martha may disagree about catch timing; Maren worries about low creek; Davy is still a boy before horror.
**Town:** produce sale, inn catering, working nets, repair quotes.
**Microstory:** first summer profit vs farm needs vs Wicker Cove roof boards.
**No-player path:** inn keeps its buckets; market buys from its existing crews.
**Sight:** sun uphill, fog downhill, grass drying inland.

### July — The letter has consequences
**Farm:** hot afternoons, ordinary harvest; copied knock possible under authored conditions; earliest mill meeting day300.
**People:** vicar and Maren confront records, postman carries original secret.
**Town:** normal trade continues despite what farmer learns; avoid forcing every worker into main climax.
**Microstory:** if inn room repaired, something changes visibly; if not, it still leaks but may be patched later.
**No-player path:** no Wicker Cove task can block `maren_book`+`vicar_register`.
**Sight:** repaired board among weathered siding, lamps at normal dusk.

### August — What stays fixed
**Farm:** prepare pantry, improve workshop, build if resources allow.
**People:** Davy has ordinary pond moments; postman talks about dry road.
**Town:** one successful repair stays; people have new bills next week.
**Microstory:** character remembers a **specific** contract or lack of visit with no condemnation.
**No-player path:** the town doesn't collapse just because player preferred their own homestead.
**Sight:** crates more regularly stacked if business supplied; old docks still old.

### September — Looking back
**Farm:** harvest, existing Harvest Moon Sept22 + story meeting logic, optional first-year 'My Days' diary based on verified work.
**People:** Maren handles old vote, vicar book, postman a usual route; Davy's day370 climax is **next month** if conditions apply.
**Town:** inn may have one lit guest room; fog still present; no town-wide victory scene.
**Microstory:** one small returned item or familiar line changes its meaning after a year.
**No-player path:** an independent player can have a meaningful farm-only diary.
**Sight:** autumn rains begin, garden is cut back, workshop ready for winter.

## Second calendar autumn and post-ending rhythm
- October1–4: ordinary work continues without a title card that resets relationships.
- October5 (conditional): original Davy night has complete priority; optional earlier memories color interpretation rather than timer or outcome.
- Oct6–30: ongoing everyday work, preparing for the crow bell; residents still trade, cook, keep books.
- October31 onward: original bell/grove path and one of three ending events according to original flags. Wicker Cove fog does not clear automatically after any ending.
- **After ending:** resume seasonal rhythm; one household/business line can reflect actual prior work. The world remains a place to live, not a set of expired quests.

## Recurring character work (first town implementation target)
A whole game day is 420 real seconds; town errands should permit returning to farm. Prefer anchored activities/short visual pose swaps, **not** simulation of every footstep.
- Edith: kitchen/open inn → laundry/sheet repair → guestroom if unlocked → close hearth.
- Thomas: inventory opening → counter sales → deliveries/stock note → tally.
- Gideon: check nets → fish/boat work → sort catch with Martha → moor.
- Martha: receive and sort catch → market sales → preserve stock → close ledger.
Each routine can exist as 2–3 distinct states; use real opening hours audited during implementation. Ensure ordinary shopping possible even when owner moves temporarily.
Existing walker schedules are **already defined** by `FolkSystem` and must remain independent; do not apply this proposed town schedule to them.

## Seasonal reactivity tiers
- **Tier A, cosmetic:** season changes art/light/work props; safe to omit if art budget constrained.
- **Tier B, factual dialogue:** NPC mentions crops, actual completed request, frost or repairs; gated by saved facts.
- **Tier C, real system difference:** available stock, working room, storage, path access actually altered. Use sparingly; this is where choices are felt.
- **Tier D, main narrative:** 17 authored chapters and endings, never randomized or controlled by local reputation.
Aim for one Tier C state change per authored town arc, with a few Tier A/B references. Do not confuse dozens of seasonal variants with meaningful depth.

## Anti-content-bloat safeguards
- No month requires a new minigame or grand festival; existing canonical event rewards remain.
- No auto-start NPC cutscenes during critical night encounter or save transition.
- Don't add daily house chores just to prove world is alive.
- Don't spawn full-screen new HUD counters for town goodwill, rumors or prosperity.
- Avoid making loneliness automatically a failure state. Quiet farmer and cautious explorer remain supported life choices.
- If a town worker has dialogue for the season but player does not go to town, that content can simply remain unseen; don't force a letter summarizing everything.
