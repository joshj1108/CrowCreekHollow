## The winter question
What do you keep, what can you spare, and who gets to ask? Survival is not a charity exam. The farmer's own hunger and heat matter. A neighbor's loss also matters. Both truths stand together.

Winter narrative quality depends on non-supernatural scenes: a stove that consumes real fuel; a letter carried through cold rain; a child trying to do a small good thing badly; an older neighbor who is not always right. The mystery may intrude rarely but should not take over ordinary winter days.

### Winter architecture: time is precious
- 420 real seconds/day. Do not force multi-screen dialogue in a freezing / low-comfort situation, block heating inputs, consume critical hours while text is open, or make the player miss an existing story event because they read mail.
- Scenes should be player-opened at a fireplace/table/diary/mailbox or triggered in safe conversation.
- All donated, paid, carried, sold or cooked items must come from actual game resources and transactions. Proposed contract scenes below should fallback gracefully to plain dialogue if town/inn systems do not yet exist.
- Nothing in these scenes changes the original Rook chapter path, winter weather rules, Davy day370, original town letter or ending gates.

These lines require an actual stage/art or repair fact. They are not generic rewards for selecting dialogue.

# THE FIVE WINTER LETTERS — PLACEMENT AND DIFFERENT HUMAN PRESSURES

The original full winter scenes are already in `ROAD_TO_CROW_CREEK_HOLLOW_BRANCHING_SCREENPLAY_DRAFT.md`. Preserve that script. The connective tissue below defines **how the farmer lives with the letters**, not replacement excerpts.

| Origin | Letter brings an external event | The farmer's first small practical response | What must stay unresolved until spring/summer |
|---|---|---|---|
| A / Cost of Room | Old address, stored boxes or account has changed | Read/file, acknowledge receipt, choose to ask for costs/details | Actual shipment/release/account settlement decision |
| B / Work Belonged to Everyone | Old workplace encountered a concrete issue or has found its own solution | Answer briefly, ask what help is needed, or decline | Whether to accept bounded paid work, consult once or close contact |
| C / House With One Less Person | Shared object, key, routine or arrangement changes | Decide whether to keep, reply or request clarity | A concrete later boundary/transfer/contact decision |
| D / Place in Photograph | Someone from prior life asks what journey really revealed | Choose what to disclose now or postpone | Whether to share a true visited experience, invite contact or keep privacy |
| E / Chance Before It Closed | Time-sensitive earlier opportunity moves toward expiry | Confirm receipt and deadline, request exact terms | Final decision about claim, extension or relinquishment |

No sender is entitled to an immediate answer. No-contact is a valid privacy state, though a documented official account can still send ordinary mail. A player not carrying a keepsake must still get meaningful origin continuity without the game pretending an item was packed.

## Scene W-08, "Keeping Enough"
**[TRIGGER]** Optional bounded genuine request from an NPC or future village service. Existing winter supply scarcity must be actually checked.

**[IF REQUEST EXISTS]**
> "We're short," the note said. "Paying the regular rate. Take what you need to keep your own stove lit."

4. **Defer with a true deadline** → honest expiration, not an invented loss.

**[IF REQUEST NOT IMPLEMENTED]** Do not show the note. Winter ordinary chores are enough until the actual town/inn systems exist.

**[LATER CALLBACK]** Only a truly completed delivery may influence availability of a meal, inn guest stay, or one specific resident's next line. A future inn built from drafts alone cannot produce current-game callbacks.

# A SMALL HUMAN REVERSAL TO CARRY INTO SPRING

In October, Maren cautions the farmer about the leaking house. In January, if her own real source-lore personal limitation supports it, she might accept a practical suggestion in return—perhaps a safer fuel arrangement or dry storage. Her response may be amused or reluctant. The reversal is not that the player 'saved' a wise old woman. It is that someone who gives advice can also receive it.

**MAREN (only after actual offered practical suggestion)**
> "I suppose that's sensible."
>
> "You don't sound pleased."
>
> "I don't see why a thing needs to be pleasing to be sensible."

Do not imply implementation, resolved Ruth mystery or a heart threshold effect unless audited against the source.
