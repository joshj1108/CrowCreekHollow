# CCH-CHARACTERS-002 — People of the Hollow: Character and Relationship Bible
## Independent lives, dialogue voice, scenes and irreversible limits

**2026-11-14. Literary development only.** Original character dialogue, heart thresholds, locations, `FolkSystem` flags and `StoryAct` are protected. Candidate townspeople and all new sample scenes here are **not approved canon**. This document synthesizes `CHARACTER_LIVES_AND_RELATIONSHIPS_DESIGN.md`, `RESIDENT_ARCS_TWO_YEAR_BLUEPRINT.md`, `SCENE_ANTHOLOGY_AND_CHARACTER_VOICE_BIBLE.md`, `LIVING_COMMUNITY_RELATIONSHIP_WEB.md`, `WICKER_COVE_LIVED_IN_DISTRICT_BIBLE.md` and `NARRATIVE_VOICE_AND_LITERARY_STANDARDS.md`. **Do not replace source scenes with these samples.**

# I. The test for a person
Every named resident should be able to answer four questions without mentioning the player:
1. What would they do on an ordinary Tuesday?
2. Whose interests can they disagree with, and still respect?
3. What do they want that the farmer cannot simply purchase?
4. What changes in their day if the farmer never speaks to them?

Each also needs a specific voice—word length, humor, omissions, practical vocabulary, how they ask for help, how they respond to a boundary—and at least one behavior unconnected to the mystery. A person exists before and after dialogue. No resident should tell the player who they really are.

## Existing original walkers (runtime story authority)

### Old Maren — practicality and the cost of remembering
**Already established:** last cottage down lane, sister Ruth at mill, former relationship with Agnes, surviving hen line, window lamp, older bell vote, the minute book required for canonical meeting. Original source scenes `maren_lamp`, `maren_hens`, `maren_hands`, `maren_book` are irreplaceable.

**Habit:** notices leaks and weather before praising improvements; can be dryly funny. Acknowledges an exchange by its actual price and effort. Knows that a person can refuse help without being cruel.

**Ordinary want:** weatherproof enough life and a reliable practical routine. **Limit:** cannot make Ruth or the other voters remember what she remembers; cannot control player choices or old evidence.

**Voice:** clipped observations, small concrete judgments and gently barbed humor, no occult riddles. Never call her an all-knowing witch.

**Sample optional scene — 'The Empty Hook' [after actual farm repair]**
> "That hook used to hold a lantern," Maren said.
>
> "Does it still?"
>
> "Holds a coat well enough."
>
> She tested it with two fingers.
>
> "You don't have to put everything back the way it was. Only the things that still work."

**Possible later factual callback:** If player actually mounted/hung something on an existing hook, Maren notices its utility. No new prop claimed in production unless scene art exists.

**Relationship with Ruth:** two people protecting history through different labor: Ruth keeps written records, Maren keeps the lamp and hens. They can quarrel over a soup recipe without the plot declaring one truthful and one dishonest. Ruth is not automatically a new physical NPC.

### Davy Tull — an ordinary boy in an extraordinary place
**Already established:** Mrs Tull, grandfather in Elias's orchard, missing dog Pip, pond stones, two-note whistle, special second-autumn day370 20:00–01:00 encounter, `davy_turn` and later conditional story outcomes. Do not promise his survival.

**Habit:** holds the best skipping stone instead of throwing it; asks literal questions adults avoid. Humor is plain and restless, not precocious prophetic poetry.

**Ordinary want:** Pip, good play, attention without adults always turning serious. **Limit:** cannot solve others' fears or make adult decisions about original story.

**Voice:** brief questions with a practical hook, playful argument over tiny details, sometimes defensive.

**Sample optional scene — 'The Best Stone' [eligible pond meeting]**
> Davy held out two stones.
>
> "Which one?"
>
> "The flatter one."
>
> "They're both flat."
>
> "Then the one with the smooth edge."
>
> "That's the one I was saving."
>
> He put both stones in his pocket.
>
> "I'll ask someone else."

**If player leaves early:** He keeps searching; future text may not claim the player stayed to play. A second-year empty pond can mean something only because these ordinary afternoons existed.

### The vicar — service without absolution on command
**Already established:** removed bell, clapper, chapel and register, rope marks/remorse, empty services, authored scenes leading to `vicar_register`. The register must be encountered by original prerequisites, not a new favor check.

**Habit:** cleans a room even when no one comes; needs things for the chapel; takes responsibility for an old choice without instantly offering the correct explanation.

**Ordinary want:** maintain a place for anyone who needs it, even when attendance is poor. **Limit:** cannot undo bell removal by speech or transfer guilt to farmer.

**Voice:** measured, careful with truth, rarely jokes except at his own expense. Avoid sermonizing or high fantasy pronouncements.

**Sample optional scene — 'A Place Set'**
> There were two cups on the chapel table.
>
> "Are you expecting somebody?"
>
> "Not particularly."
>
> "Then why two?"
>
> "It seems unkind to have only one if someone comes."

No magical implication to second cup; it demonstrates character through a small act.

### The postman — privacy and staying on the route
**Already established:** decades of deliveries, retained Rook correspondence, final handwriting reveal `post_hand`, old Lowmere postal/iron references needing unresolved geography audit. Do not create any early clue to handwriting or privately read post.

**Habit:** keeps envelopes dry; treats undelivered mail as unfinished work, not treasure. Has boundaries about private words.

**Ordinary want:** finish a route properly; be trusted with things not meant for him. **Limit:** deliveries may be late or addresses no longer occupied; he cannot repair every family separation.

**Voice:** calm, spare, wryly exact about dates and conditions; an ethical boundary expressed in one short sentence.

**Sample optional scene — 'Wrong Address'**
> "This one's come back," he said.
>
> "Is the address wrong?"
>
> "Not wrong. Old."
>
> "Where does it go now?"
>
> "Back to the person who wrote it, if there's still someone there."

Only use if an actual non-story mail-return route exists; not the canonical Rook correspondence.

# II. Four working village candidates — identities NOT owner-finalized

The following names and roles remain candidate material from November 14 design. Confirm with owner before production art, dialogue, quests, contracts or persistent saves. Their conflicts should make community recovery believable without making the farmer the center of every life.

### Edith Vale — accommodation is labor, not a background decoration
**Proposed work:** operates a struggling inn; cleans, keeps bookings and tries to make a dry room possible.
**Ordinary Tuesday:** launders sheets, checks leaks, remembers which visitor needs a quiet room; deals with spoiling provisions.
**Want:** reliable guest accommodation without pretending the whole town has recovered.
**Limit:** cannot accept an unsafe room as payment, nor make every guest stay.
**Voice:** brisk, clear requests, soft courtesy when someone does real work; not endlessly grateful.

**Sample — 'The Morning Room'**
> Edith opened the shutters and found the basin was half full.
>
> "That's from one night," she said.
>
> "You could move the bed."
>
> "I did. Twice."
>
> [She looks at the player, not pleading.]
>
> "I need the window fixed. Tell me what it costs before you start."

**Choices:** fair stated contract; offer material exchange; defer. Actual repair visually changes room, and only a subsequent authentic booking creates lit/occupied-window echo. If player declines, she pursues another option rather than waiting forever.

### Thomas Bellweather — ledgers record people as well as money
**Proposed work:** accounts/shop, orders and receipts; overpromises when trying to keep goods moving.
**Ordinary Tuesday:** compares invoices, counts small change, corrects an order and perhaps apologizes.
**Want:** enough predictable trade to keep a business honest.
**Limit:** cannot promise same goods twice or make promises for the farmer.
**Voice:** precise numbers, self-corrections and reluctant admissions; specific rather than theatrical merchant patter.

**Sample — 'The Pencil Line'**
> "I wrote five," Thomas said.
>
> Edith looked at the crate.
>
> "There are four."
>
> "Then one of us is wrong."
>
> "Or someone already took one."
>
> Thomas counted again.
>
> "That would make three possibilities."

**Choice:** farmer can check an actual delivery receipt, offer replacement at a price, or leave. The characters continue solving it if ignored.

### Gideon Pike — the work of coming home
**Proposed work:** fishing boat/cove operations and weather judgment.
**Ordinary Tuesday:** checks lines, hull and crew, says no to an unsafe departure.
**Want:** a reliable trip that feeds the crew and earns enough for the next one.
**Limit:** cannot sail out every day; cannot override dangerous visibility because the player wants an item.
**Voice:** few words, practical caution; no boastful pirate diction.

**Sample — 'A Safer Tide'**
> Gideon looked toward the harbor mouth.
>
> "You going out?" the farmer asked.
>
> "Not yet."
>
> "It looks calm."
>
> "From here."
>
> He tightened the knot on a line that did not need tightening.
>
> "Come back after noon."

A refusal is not a secret quest failure. New boat voyages remain an optional later system after the farm/town loop works.

### Martha Fenwick — a meal must arrive before it spoils
**Proposed work:** fish supply, household food, seasonal shortages and market coordination.
**Ordinary Tuesday:** sorts catch, bargains, sets aside something for her own table, asks what the inn actually needs.
**Want:** to get food used fairly and keep her household supplied.
**Limit:** cannot keep everything fresh or give away stock indefinitely.
**Voice:** direct, economical, warm in action more than praise.

**Sample — 'The Good One'**
> "That one's for supper," she said.
>
> "I thought it was for sale."
>
> "It was this morning."
>
> She put the fish into another crate.
>
> "I have to eat too."

Do not moralize the fact that a business owner feeds herself.

# III. People connected to other people

Relationships matter when they alter routine independently of player input. The following are **design possibilities**, not declarations that new locations/characters are implemented.

| Relationship | Small daily friction | Practical scene/response | What remains independent of player |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maren / Ruth | Accounts versus practical memory | Wrong soup recipe, precise receipt | Sisters disagree and still look after each other |
| Davy / Mrs Tull | Play versus household rules | Muddy boots or late return | Family cares for Davy even if farmer ignores him |
| Davy / grandfather | Stories of orchard labor | Old tool/stone anecdote | Grandfather's experience is his own |
| Vicar / postman | A record kept versus a letter delivered | Two different versions of a date | Neither becomes a cheap exposition source |
| Edith / Thomas | Promised flour and late deliveries | Inn meal changes when supply actually arrives | They argue, adjust, pay others |
| Gideon / Martha | Weather risk versus food demand | Fish availability changes after a safe trip | They negotiate without player |
| Edith / Martha | A guest meal's timing | A real paid order and served dinner | Inn can buy elsewhere when farmer declines |
| Thomas / households | Short credit and honest payment | A receipt can change access/trust in a specific account | No town-wide good/bad reputation score |

**NPC knowledge provenance:** visible work, public sale, actual dialogue, delivered letter, plausible trusted messenger. If a resident cannot know a private farmer origin, they must not mention it. Residents do not magically detect the player's hidden attributes.

# IV. Four micro-stories to prove earned continuity

### Micro-story 1 — The roof is fixed, but a neighbor's opinion changes
**Act 1:** Maren offers advice/trade/independence.  
**Action:** Player actually repairs the correct roof/wall element.  
**Callback:** Maren comments on visible work, using the chosen approach.  
**Later:** The repaired house appears dry or more weathered *as art/state allows*.  
**Failure:** A generic "you've done well" line when player did nothing.  
**Source gate:** source day4 trek and `maren_book` unchanged.

### Micro-story 2 — A child grows familiar without being saved by a dialogue option
**Act 1:** Davy counts skips and keeps a stone.  
**Action:** Optional conversation witnessed.  
**Callback:** Davy mentions the stone at another eligible source visit, not as a new magical item.  
**Later:** Day370 is source-owned and may still go either way.  
**Failure:** Relationship heart score auto-rescues him or author claims he lived when actual flag says lost.

### Micro-story 3 — The inn's upstairs window has an earned light
**Act 1:** Edith specifies water leak and fair material/cash cost.  
**Action:** Player genuinely fixes window through approved repair system.  
**Callback:** Inn can offer room on safe weather; a real guest booking can later show light.  
**Later:** A guest needs food/clean sheets; Edith remains at work.  
**Failure:** Light or room occupancy appears just because player promised to help.

### Micro-story 4 — A person from the old life doesn't wait forever
**Act 1:** Origin winter letter gives sender's real problem/deadline.  
**Action:** Spring reply chooses assistance, a boundary, payment or closure.  
**Callback:** Sender acknowledges *actual* decision; might solve the problem independently.  
**Later:** September diary and second-autumn shelf/letter/ledger reflect one true persistent state.  
**Failure:** Every origin receives same sentimental "home is here" message or an NPC knows things never sent.

# V. Voice and quality gate
Before any dialogue goes into production, review:
- Can you identify the speaker with name removed? If not, revise for concrete priorities and rhythm.
- Does this person have a task in the scene? If not, find one or shorten the exchange.
- Is the scene saying something about character through action, rather than a speech about its own theme?
- Would it still make sense if the player had never interacted with that resident? If so, maybe it shouldn't pretend intimacy.
- Did the player really complete the triggering action? Is the callback conditional on genuine completion and state?
- Did an NPC react with realistic dignity to refusal?
- Does anything reveal Rook evidence, postman handwriting, fog cause, or Davy's future too early?
- Will this still fit a short 420-second game day with accessibility and player choice?
- Does every arc survive each of the three original endings and post-ending free play without invented reunions?
