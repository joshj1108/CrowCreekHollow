# PART I — SEPTEMBER, ONE YEAR LATER

## Scene F-01 "The Second List"
**[Trigger]** First September, player voluntarily uses table/diary/market ledger at a safe time. At least one true farm action recorded; if none, neutral baseline.

**BASELINE**
> It took longer to count what you had kept than what you had sold.
>
> You crossed out one item, then wrote it again on the next page.
>
> There would be another season to plan for.

**[IF VERIFIED REPAIR]**
> A year ago the boards around the window had let in the rain.
>
> You wrote down what the repair had cost.
>
> There was no reason to guess next time.

**[IF VERIFIED MARKET ACTIVITY]**
> You copied the week's sales under the old figures.
>
> Some crops had earned more than others.
>
> The numbers were useful. They did not tell the whole story.

**[IF NO VERIFIED SALE]**
> You made a new column for seed and supplies.
>
> The empty space could stay empty until it was needed.

**[Editorial constraint]** The phrase "a year ago" only applies after actual in-game elapsed year and appropriate visual/progression state. If a September entry after eleven months is not a full year, use "last autumn." Month-to-calendar correctness must be tested in game; don't assert a completed exact year early.

## Scene F-02 "Two Pages"
**[Trigger]** Origin-specific late-year reflection; player prior-life events/decisions actually stored; no fixed mandatory cinematic.

**NARRATION**
> There were two pages you kept turning back to.
>
> The first had come with you along the road.
>
> The second was made of work you had done here.

**[ONE ORIGIN-SPECIFIC FACT, selected only if verifiable]**
- A / housing: The old housing account is settled; OR the shipped box is still on its way; OR the player has let the stored items go; OR there is no box route. Do not combine mutually exclusive variants.
- B / work: a limited consultation was paid/completed; OR one answer sent; OR the old contact closed; OR an offer remains pending.
- C / household: a shared object was retained, returned, or held for later; a relationship boundary was stated or a correspondence kept. Never invent bereavement or an apology.
- D / photograph: specific place visited and described truthfully; or no reply sent; or player still comparing the photo to what they have seen.
- E / chance: former opportunity closed, extended on documented terms, or allowed to lapse with known consequence.
**[ONE FARM FACT]** actual crop harvest, tool built, woodland logs hewn, roof repair, valid resident delivery, or actual story evidence. No phantom assets.

**PLAYER CHOSEN PRIVATE REFLECTION**
- "I have changed more than the house has."
- "The house has changed more than I have."
- "I don't need to decide that now."
- "Keep the facts. Leave the rest unwritten."

These lines are *interpretations explicitly chosen by the player*, not the diary's automatic judgment. A person can still find their prior life meaningful without declaring the farm a cure.

# PART IV — PERSONAL-LIFE AFTERWORDS BY ORIGIN

These are **not** substitutions for original epilogues. They are potential mailbox/diary/house object echoes, only after corresponding origin decisions exist and are recorded. Multiple endings share the same human history without inventing separate realities.

### A — A room and a deed
**If boxes were shipped and delivered:**
> The box had been under the table so long that it left a pale rectangle when you moved it.
>
> You opened it and set one ordinary thing on the shelf.

**If boxes released:**
> The last address on the envelope was crossed out.
>
> You put the letter away.

**If no boxes / housing ledger:**
> There was no more to pay at the old address.
>
> The new house still had expenses of its own.

Owning a purchased town deed, if obtained, is a separate legal fact; it does not automatically resolve the earlier housing story or signify moral healing.

### B — Work that no longer waits
**If bounded paid advice completed:**
> The payment came with a short note.
>
> "Sorted it. Thank you."
>
> It was signed by someone still doing the work.

**If refused:**
> There were no new work letters that month.
>
> You had given them an answer. They had accepted it.

Do not narrate that former colleagues are helpless or cruel; they continue.

### C — A house with one less person
**If shared object returned:**
> The empty space on the shelf was easier to see before the other things gathered around it.

**If retained:**
> The thing had been there all winter.
>
> Sometimes you used it. Sometimes it stayed where you put it.

No mandatory breakup, death or grief resolution. The specific object must exist in chosen origin branch.

### D — A place in a photograph
**If the player shared a true scene:**
> The letter came back with a question about the road.
>
> You knew the answer now. You had walked it.

**If private:**
> The photograph remained where you had put it.
>
> No one had asked to see it again.

Avoid implying the photo predicted the Hollow or contained a supernatural clue.

### E — A chance before it closed
**If claim settled:**
> The final paper arrived without ceremony.
>
> You filed it with the others.

**If allowed to lapse:**
> The date passed.
>
> There was no reply to write, and no reason to pretend the choice had not been made.

**If extension negotiated:** show the actual future date, not a permanent unresolved countdown invented without mechanics.

# PART V — THE FINAL SCENE SHOULD BELONG TO THE PLAYER

### Proposed reusable closing frame: "The Door"
**[Trigger]** Player voluntarily returns indoors after an original ending; this is a *post-ending reflection* and not a new credited finale. Must be anchored to actual rendering/inventory/interior stage.

> The latch caught once before the door closed.
>
> You put down what you had carried in.
>
> The table was where it had always been.
>
> There was a place to put tomorrow's work.

**[Then select at most two verified details, NOT a universal speech]**
- A particular repair held in the weather.
- A letter from the player's actual earlier-life branch lay on the table.
- A genuine inn order or farm sale was written in a ledger.
- A small object was present only if acquired.
- The window was lit only if the house's actual light was burning.
- The path Maren once took could be seen beyond the fence only if the world/layout actually matches.

**[Optional player-authored line, can be left blank]**
- "There is still a great deal to do."
- "I know where to begin tomorrow."
- "I don't intend to stay forever."
- "I think I'll stay a little longer."
- "Nothing more tonight."

This is the game's thematic wager: the player can be free to leave, stay, remain uncertain, or simply finish another day's work. A 'true ending' is not reserved for social virtue or universal belonging.
