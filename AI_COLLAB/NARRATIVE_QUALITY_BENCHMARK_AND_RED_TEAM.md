# CCH-STORY-QUALITY-001 — Narrative Quality Benchmark, Red-Team Review and Playtest Protocol

**2026-11-14 — Editorial/production quality standard; not implementation approval.**
**Aspirational user target:** Crow Creek Hollow should compete for players' lasting emotional attention with the finest stories, but not by claiming success in advance. The proper comparison is craft quality: memorable character choices, honest causation, surprising recontextualization, meaningful uncertainty, coherent themes, beautiful readable prose and satisfying interactive life. These standards must be **tested in-game with real players**.

## I. The hard problems to solve

### 1. Ambition without pretension
**Risk:** every scene tries to be poetic, so characters all sound alike and ordinary farming feels like an allegory.  
**Counter:** 80% of dialogue does useful human work: weather, money, a joke, a name, meal, safety, materials. Deep scenes arrive after relationships have time to grow. Delete sentences that merely announce themes.

### 2. A cozy life game that is not a disguised cutscene novel
**Risk:** a farmer cannot finish work because dialogue interrupts constantly.  
**Counter:** ordinary game systems remain rewarding without story. Major interactions voluntary or organically triggered by existing original chapters; brief scene lengths, skippable dialog where safely possible. The shortest strong scene is preferred to a longer weak one.

### 3. A dark mystery that isn't a bait-and-switch
**Risk:** the promised cozy farm is constantly terrifying, or the game hints at mysteries never paid off.  
**Counter:** distinguish Rook arc (answered through existing 17 chapters/3 outcomes) from Wicker Cove fog (deliberately unresolved onset/cause). Show farm competence growth and restorative activities. Hard horror arrives at existing milestones, not every sunny morning.

### 4. Meaningful choices without the 'perfect altruist' route
**Risk:** helping every villager always earns more money, best ending, all friendships and prestige.  
**Counter:** real time/material cost, competing projects, legitimate fair payment, self-preservation, character autonomy. No universal 'good person' flag. Some endings require specific evidence and risks, not kindness scores.

### 5. Consequences without punishing curiosity or independence
**Risk:** one choice irreversibly locks core story or forces players to meet an NPC before they can survive.  
**Counter:** optional arcs can be missed, delayed and materially unequal, but survival, baseline timber, first lantern, original walkers, day240 letter and canonical endings remain source-compatible. Davy one-night miss has existing real stakes—don't invent another hidden-child-safety meter.

### 6. Player identity without mind-reading
**Risk:** auto-diary says 'I regret' or 'I loved them' after a paid exchange, or implies the player visited a town they didn't.  
**Counter:** event ledger of verifiable actions and explicit chosen interpretations only; conditional lines omit unproved facts; old save falls back to neutral events.

### 7. A complete Rook mystery versus endless vague metaphors
**Risk:** everything is suggestive but nothing becomes clear.  
**Counter:** source's original main plot has specific records, decisions, Davy's stakes, orchard, grove, and endings. Allow canonical facts to resolve at their scheduled scenes. Withhold fog origin, not the entirety of every plot.

### 8. Ending hierarchy that overrides the game's philosophical goals
**Risk:** presentation implies Two Came Home is morally 'true' and every other path is incorrect.  
**Counter:** don't fake equivalence of consequences—the Elias return has more specific achievements—but keep all three authored outcomes consequential, continuing, and worthy of narrative treatment. No scoreboard rates the player as good/bad. Recoverable Hollow path remains real, not a fail screen.

### 9. A town that exists only while player is nearby
**Risk:** Edith's buckets never move, fishermen never fish, and the entire port waits indefinitely for one carpenter.  
**Counter:** small, deterministic offscreen contingencies: alternate supplier, partial owner repair, seasonally shifted work. Don't simulate a giant economy. Show one repeated work action per resident and occasional independent decisions.

### 10. Cast expansion that dilutes the original four
**Risk:** twenty new named Wicker Cove NPCs eclipse Maren, Davy, the vicar and postman before their arcs work.  
**Counter:** first pair of new approved residents only, a single two-person interaction, reuse existing relationship systems. Four original walkers retain their core scenes/heart conditions.

### 11. Plot holes from naming and calendar changes
**Risk:** blindly replacing old Lowmere with Wicker Cove changes the postman's bell-source geography, or the "second-year" storyline is postponed to October although day240 letter already exists.  
**Counter:** run the `CCH-LORE-NAME-001` exact source audit; owner chooses geographical reconciliation; preserve source-time day240 letter, conditional orchard, knock, meeting, day370 Davy and second All Hallows, 17 chapter progression and 3 endings.

### 12. Repetitive crafting that undermines meaning
**Risk:** logs→planks intended to feel physical becomes a dozen station trips, making every roof repair a chore.  
**Counter:** nearwood obvious and renewable, one useful log-haul batch, bench accessible from beginning, reasonable conversion and input duration, future anvil after stone tools; old saves safe, fuels remain obtainable. Work is tactile, not grind.

## II. Scene grading: twelve criteria (0/1/2 each)

Score each flagship scene from 0 to 2:
1. **Literal clarity** — a player can say what happened without interpreting a metaphor.
2. **Distinct voice** — character could not be swapped for another without rewriting.
3. **Specificity** — one memorable physical detail tied to this person/place.
4. **Agency** — player could act, decline, or respond through gameplay where appropriate.
5. **Honest stakes** — the cost/time/uncertainty is understandable.
6. **Causal consequence** — at least one result beyond cosmetic branch, or scene properly identified as ambient.
7. **Subtext** — feelings are not simply narrated or labeled.
8. **Structural purpose** — changes relationship, understanding, routine or question; not filler.
9. **Continuity** — all claims and ages/events support original source history.
10. **Pacing** — works in the actual 420-second day and screen format.
11. **Restraint** — no flood of ominous riddles, fake twist, fog answer or melodrama.
12. **Replay truth** — alternate/no-town/old-save runs don't witness facts they never earned.

For a flagship optional scene, target at least **20/24** with zeroes forbidden for clarity, continuity and replay truth. A short atmospheric moment may legitimately score low on choice/causal consequence but should not masquerade as a major quest. **These are internal suggested review thresholds, not an objective artistic formula**.

## III. 'Read it aloud' test
For every NPC scene, read it aloud at natural pace:
- Can someone speak these sentences without sounding as though they are giving an essay?
- Is every line doing work?
- Does one physical action stand in for a paragraph of emotion?
- Could the actor disagree with the farmer politely and remain believable?
- If the supernatural were removed, would this character still have something to do?
- Could the scene exist at wrong day, wrong place or wrong house stage? If yes, add state conditions or revise it.

**Example edit:**
Avoid: 'The stoic fisherman concealed deep concern beneath an impenetrable mask of weary stoicism.'
Prefer: 'Gideon wound the rope twice around the post. He checked the knot, then checked it again.'
Only use the second if player can actually see it.

## IV. Four story playtests with different histories

### Playtest A — Farm-centered
No future Wicker Cove visit, minimal NPC interaction. Must experience strong farm/home arc, original chapter path, an honest September diary and original endings. Nobody calls them beloved neighbor.

### Playtest B — Neighbor-centered
Walks with Maren/vicar/Davy, fulfills real requests; sees genuine small memories. Must **not** get fictional story evidence from nice choices. Meeting still depends on book/register.

### Playtest C — Paid work and trade
Completes real orders and a paid inn repair when implemented; house progression may be slower. Edith can thank player for a **job** without the script rebranding it a charitable gift. Contract paid once.

### Playtest D — Explorer and skeptical listener
Forest and original mystery treks; Wicker Cove fog records optional. Player might believe one testimony or withhold judgment; no definitive fog proof, no failed d20 locks the original story.

**Controls:** replay each with old-save missing new flags; test keyboard/gamepad, pause/unpause, time-of-day and story scene priority.

## V. Story-state and emotion QA matrix

| Situation | What may be said | What must not be assumed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A player turns down a winter food request | 'I kept the supplies for the house.' | 'I cared more about myself than the hungry family.' |
| A player accepted paid inn work but didn't finish | 'I agreed to repair the roof.' | 'The room is dry' or 'Edith paid me' unless true |
| A player buys the deed | 'I have the deed.' | 'The town lost all claims over the bell story.' |
| A player receives letter and never visits Cove | 'The parish wrote to the farm.' | 'The people I helped betrayed me.' |
| A player hears one fog witness | 'Gideon remembered the far headland.' | 'It used to be clear before the curse.' |
| A player has Maren hearts but not book flag | 'Maren knows me.' | 'I have Ruth's original meeting minute book.' |
| A player finds Davy | original found/home facts | special additional penalty or forced loving speech |
| A player misses Davy | 'He went into the wood' if actually lost | 'I killed him' or 'he can never come back' |
| A player reaches Hollow ending | original new keeper and later recovery can occur | irreversible eviction/game over |
| A player reaches Elias ending | Agnes/Elias original homecoming result | Wicker Cove coastal fog disappears |
| A player used generic current wood | original wood/material facts | invented future workbench/plank crafting |

## VII. Ten-year emotional afterlife: not ten-year mechanics
We do **not** need to simulate a decade of new NPC AI to make the game linger after a playthrough. A few consistent remembered actions and lingering questions are enough.

The desired post-game thought is not 'I selected the good ending.' It is closer to:
- 'I remember the first night that roof stopped leaking.'
- 'Maren was funny before I understood the lamp.'
- 'Davy used to whistle at the pond.'
- 'I built a room where a stranger could sleep.'
- 'I understood why the town was frightened, and I didn't agree with what it did.'
- 'I still don't know why the fog is there, and that's okay.'
These are examples of **potential player reactions**, not words inserted into their avatar's diary.

## VIII. Three release-quality gates

### Gate 1 — Does ordinary life feel good?
First 30 days: planting, nights, one logging→plank→repair, storage, basic character line, useful non-story project. A story can't compensate for frustrating crafting or inaccessible trees.

### Gate 2 — Does one human choice persist?
One real Maren or equivalent existing-farm situation: 3 valid approaches, actual consequence, later callback, accurate diary, save/load. Test with 2 different players + skip. No false personality labels.

### Gate 3 — Do original revelations land and do endings stay available?
Sim or controlled 420-day validation, exact day gates and all three original endings, plus two human playtests through at least Davy/meeting scenes; no lore retcon. Extra side content must not overcrowd the core.

## IX. Hard exclusions from initial production
- No fourth ending or forced romance.
- No mystery explanation for permanent town fog.
- No endless procedural story generator or LLM dialogue inserted into game runtime.
- No hidden moral score/identity axis that automatically makes NPCs regard farmer as hero.
- No town rescue bar, political alignment or forced social labor.
- No third-party existing music/art/story copied.
- No thousands of lines of dialogue before usable first town.
- No rewrite of official current story events just to fit a newly invented dramatic beat.
