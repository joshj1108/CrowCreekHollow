# CCH-SCRIPT-SPRING-SUMMER-002 — The Road Down, The Work Between
## First spring and summer: field, village, letters and the cost of a useful day

**Created:** 2026-11-14. **State:** ORIGINAL EDITORIAL EXPANSION — not current gameplay, approved dialogue, or authorization to implement town assets. Companions: `FIRST_SPRING_BRANCHING_DETAIL.md`, `FIRST_SUMMER_BRANCHING_DETAIL.md`, `ROAD_TO_CROW_CREEK_HOLLOW_SPRING_RETURN_SCREENPLAY.md`, `WICKER_COVE_LIVED_IN_DISTRICT_BIBLE.md`, `INN_ROOM_BRANCHING_STORY_PROTOTYPE.md`, `WICKER_COVE_POSTAL_LORE_CONTINUITY_AUDIT.md`, `YEAR_ONE_BRANCHING_NARRATIVE_DESIGN.md` and `INTEGRATED_NARRATIVE_BIBLE_VOL_1.md`.

**Creative objective:** Let the farmer become competent enough to choose where competence goes. A player can feed themselves, profit honestly, keep to the hillside, investigate the older mystery, or put time and materials into a struggling community. The world should remember a specific deed, not praise a generalized 'good person.' Existing Chapter 11+ events, day240 E. Pell letter, orchard rise, meeting requirements and Davy's later outcome are inviolable.

## ACT S1 — THE FIRST CLEAR MORNING (March)
### S1-01 "Warm Soil"
**[Trigger]** Player first deliberately tills/plants in spring after winter survival; no fixed mandatory date.

> The ground had softened along the fence.
>
> The first turn of earth was heavy. The second broke into smaller pieces.
>
> You put the seeds where the light would reach them.

**[If the player built genuine repaired field beds]**
> The furrows held their shape better this year.

**[If the player still uses unmodified ground]**
> A little water ran across the patch. You opened a narrow channel with the hoe.

No free seeds, crops or stat changes from reading text. The ordinary action IS the progression. A later shop or resident may only acknowledge a sale if they really bought goods.

### S1-02 "A Map of the Hill"
**[Trigger]** A farm-road sign/route access description after the town's first district has been physically implemented and approved. This is not an automatic village unlock merely because a design document exists.

> The road ran past the gate, bent around the ridge and dropped away between the trees.
>
> Somewhere below, a boat horn sounded once.
>
> The air smelled different on the downhill side.

Avoid the phrase 'the fog began a hundred years ago' or any fixed explanation. The old Lowmere letter/bell-iron geography is pending an explicit owner reconciliation; do not script a contrary postal origin. A road may pass over a ridge before descending toward a cove; that physical choice is still awaiting owner approval.

**Player can:**
- follow a real village trade contract;
- go downhill out of curiosity;
- stay on the farm to plant and repair.
Base farm success never depends on being morally 'neighborly.' No false 'first town visit' journal if town unavailable.

### S1-03 "The List" — candidate Edith / Thomas first contact
**[Trigger]** First functioning Wicker Cove district, owner-approved resident identities and working shop/inn contract. These names remain provisional until then.

**THOMAS** [at counter, checking entries]
> "I wrote it all down so I wouldn't forget."
>
> [He turns the ledger.]
> "Now I can't read what I wrote."

**EDITH**
> "I can read it. You promised the flour twice."

**THOMAS**
> "I promised the same flour to two people. That's different."

**EDITH**
> "Not to the people waiting."

**PLAYER RESPONSE**
- "Which delivery has the earlier date?" → learn actual ledger order.
- "I might have something to trade." → view actual inventory-based contract.
- "I'll leave you to sort it out." → no penalty; they resolve/renegotiate the issue offscreen under a legitimate schedule.

**[If player fulfills an actual contract]**
> Thomas crosses one entry off the page rather than delivering a universal speech of gratitude.
>
> "That makes the morning simpler," Edith says.

**[If player doesn't]**
> On the next visit, the counter may show a different supplier or a reduced menu. Never imply the village starved because the farmer didn't arrive.

**Purpose:** The village works independently. Needs are mundane and persistent. Repair is not an instant visual purification of fog, nor a moral rating.

## ACT S2 — THE ANSWER TO AN OLD LETTER (April–early June)
### S2-01 "At the Table"
**[Trigger]** Player chooses to open a verified personal origin letter and begin its substantial spring reply scene. Full per-origin text and options already exist in `ROAD_TO_CROW_CREEK_HOLLOW_SPRING_RETURN_SCREENPLAY.md`. Do not replace that document.

> You drew the chair close enough to write.
>
> The paper had been folded twice. There was room at the bottom for an answer.
>
> Outside, something scraped along the fence in the wind.

**[INTERACTION]** Load exactly one of the five original origin-specific reply scenes, including its actual earlier departure route and privacy state. E.g., 'stored boxes' cannot be used if no boxes were stored; paid former work cannot be offered if earlier correspondence explicitly closed the relationship. Any cost to mail or ship must be transparently stated and affordable/recoverable. Send only on an actual confirmation action; 'considering' is not 'sent.'

**[After an actual answer]**
> You folded the page and wrote the address.
>
> The rest depended on the person who would open it.

**[After defer]**
> You left the paper beside the ledger.
>
> It would still need an answer, or a decision not to give one.

**[After deliberate decline/closure]**
> You put the envelope away.
>
> There were things you would not ask that life to carry any longer.

The final line may imply motive; **prefer player-selected interpretation** or a strict neutral alternative: "You marked the matter closed in the notebook." Never assert healing.

### S2-02 "The Clerk's Letter" (May 28, day240)
**[IMPORTANT]** The original E. Pell parish/town letter, its actual trigger and later 'shown' flag belong entirely to `StoryAct`. This scene is **not** a newly authored replacement. The only possible addition is a neutral surrounding observation after the original letter has appeared.

### S2-03 "What They Remember About Fog"
**[Trigger]** Optional village visit with two independently confirmed residents **only after** the district and those residents exist. Keep contradictory testimony as testimony, not fact.

**VILLAGER ONE** [carrying a box]
> "It was foggy when my mother was a girl."
>
> [They set the box down.]
> "She said it wasn't always like that."

**VILLAGER TWO**
> "Your mother said a lot of things when the weather turned."
>
> "I remember it before I remember the harbor."

**PLAYER OPTIONS**
- "Does anyone have an old record?"
- "Could both of you be remembering different days?"
- "I'm looking for the shop, not the weather."

**RESULTS:** optional leads to *ordinary conflicting* ledgers/recollections; no verified origin date, cause or Rook connection. A resident may be annoyed that their memory is challenged, without a permanent lie/truth point. The player can disengage. The coast retains thick fog regardless of the season on the farm.

## ACT S3 — SUMMER WORK (June–August)
### S3-01 "The Weight of a Crate"
**[Trigger]** Player completes one real sale, kitchen fulfillment or household reserve action involving the same crop category and records it.

> The crate had felt light when you put it on the cart.
>
> It was heavy enough by the time you reached the end of the road.

**[Sale route]**
> Thomas counted the weight, checked the price and paid what was written.
>
> "If you bring more next week, send word first," he said. "I can't promise a buyer I haven't got."

**[Inn order route]**
> Edith opened the sack and checked the contents herself.
>
> "Good," she said. "I can make tomorrow's supper with this."
>
> A room upstairs still needed fixing.

**[Self-sufficiency route]**
> You put the food away before the hottest part of the day.
>
> A meal you kept could not also be sold. That was a choice, not a failure.

**[If no town]** Keep this scene at farm market or actual cooking interface; don't spawn Thomas/Edith or imply deliveries.

### S3-02 "The Room Above the Street"
**[Prerequisite]** Owner-approved candidate residents, functional inn interior, actual guest-room repair contract, save-persistent staging. The original detailed source `INN_ROOM_BRANCHING_STORY_PROTOTYPE.md` owns the full choices and guest narrative. Below is a short scene stub, **not** its substitute.

**EDITH**
> "The window won't close in the rain."
>
> "Do you have another room?"
>
> "I have a bed. I can't charge a traveler for sleeping in a wet one."

**PLAYER OPTIONS**
- Review actual materials and offered payment, with a real completion action.
- Propose a fair alternative supply exchange, if supported.
- Decline or defer; Edith seeks another realistic option.

**[After true completed repair]**
> The window shut without needing a wedge.
>
> The room smelled of wood and fresh linen.
>
> By evening there was a light upstairs.

**[If unfinished]**
> There was still a board propped against the door. Edith had put a basin beneath the leak.

**[After later actual guest stay]**
> A small pair of muddy boots stood neatly outside the room.
>
> Edith had crossed out tomorrow's vacancy.

No invented inn revenue, guest or glowing window before the mechanic/scene exists. No town magically restored by a single repair.

### S3-03 "Half a Crate"
**[Trigger]** Actual fish/produce order or resident availability in approved seaside district; Martha and Thomas remain candidates.

**MARTHA**
> "This one won't last until tomorrow."
>
> [She lifts a fish by the tail.]
> "You can pay me for it now or I can cook it for my own supper."

**THOMAS**
> "You'd rather sell it."
>
> "I'd rather it got eaten," she says. "That's not quite the same."

**PLAYER RESPONSE / ACTION**
- Buy at displayed fair price if recipe/cooking exists.
- Take a valid delivery/inn order if inventory economy supports it.
- Decline, leaving Martha to eat or find another customer.

A bounded market interaction with no fabricated generosity score. The real question is what gets used before spoiling, not who is morally pure.

**[Trigger]** Only once repeatable nearwood timber trip and optional deeper stand are implemented. Never force late-game horror to get basic planks.

>

**[If player returns safely with timber]**

>

No occult surprise on every route. The forest is important because it supplies wood for survival, not because every tree hides a quest marker.

## ACT S5 — THE DAY AFTER
### S5-01 "A Return Road"
**[Trigger]** Actual first successful travel out and home after a meaningful task, before bedtime, within real play day. If player hasn't visited town, this scene may instead follow a nearwood return.

**NARRATION**
> The uphill part of the road took longer.
>
> By the time the fence came into view, the air had lost the smell of the harbor.
>
> There was the house.
>
> Whatever you had done down the hill, there was work waiting here too.

**[If only nearwood]**
> You knew the turn where the path widened.
>
> You came through the gate with enough wood to carry.
>
> The house was still where you'd left it.

**[Why it belongs]** The farmer's world expands but doesn't abandon the homestead. The road back is an emotional pattern built from ordinary commuting. It should be a satisfying, functional journey—not a second compulsory loading-screen monologue.

## ACT S6 — THE SUMMER LEDGER
### S6-01 "What Was Used"
**[Trigger]** Player voluntarily reads a diary/inventory/ledger summary near end August. Compose from true data.

**FACT CANDIDATES, each requiring recorded proof**
- Sold specific crop/contract; report amount only if stored.
- Repaired inn window; if actually restored and visual changed.
- Kept enough crop for meals; avoid reporting quantity if not tracked.
- Made planks at workbench, only when completed and saved.
- Answered/refused/deferred a personal origin obligation with precise variant.
- Actually saw orchard rise, registered evidence, or attended valid meeting.

**PLAYER CHOSEN REFLECTION (optional, don't moralize)**
- "Some of the work had to be done again."
- "I know the road better now."
- "There are still people I haven't met."
- "There are things I don't intend to finish."
- "Leave this page as a record without comment."

No player is required to conclude that community work equals belonging. The diary's job is honest memory; the player supplies meaning.
